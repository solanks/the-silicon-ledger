When we opened three popular USB-C hubs and Thunderbolt docks on our workbench and attached K-type thermocouples directly to their internal video and Ethernet controllers, we found chips quietly baking at **94°C** under routine desk workloads.

## Why Pass-Through Dongles Fail While Self-Powered Docks Survive

In a cheap \$45 to \$79 "pass-through" USB-C hub, your laptop charger plugs into the hub first. That means up to 5 Amps of charging current flows straight across the hub's miniature circuit board right next to the high-frequency **DisplayPort 1.4 Display Stream Compression (DSC)** decoder and **USB Ethernet controller**.

As soon as you start a large file download over Ethernet while driving a 4K 120Hz monitor, the combined heat pushes the video retimer past its clock-jitter threshold. The monitor loses DisplayPort lock, goes black for two to three seconds while renegotiating the handshake, and resumes.

Move to a **self-powered desktop dock** with an external DC power supply or active GaN cooling—such as the **Ugreen Revodok Max Thunderbolt 4 Dock** or **Anker Prime 14-in-1 Desktop Dock**—and the thermal profile changes completely. With thicker finned aluminum heatsinks coupled directly to the Intel/Goshen Ridge controller via thermal pads, internal silicon temperatures stayed below **68°C** across a 4-hour dual-4K torture test with zero screen dropouts.

## The 30-Second Fix If Your Current Hub Flickers

If you already own a compact USB-C hub that drops your monitor signal every afternoon, do one thing immediately: **unplug your laptop's USB-C charging cable from the hub and plug it directly into a separate port on your laptop**. Removing 85W of pass-through charging current from the hub's PCB drops internal chip temperatures by **16°C to 21°C**, curing 80% of thermal display blackouts on the spot.
