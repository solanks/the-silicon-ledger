If you have ever boarded a six-hour transcontinental flight with a 16-inch MacBook Pro (`99.6Wh` internal battery) and a brand-new **"27,650mAh / 99.5Wh Airline-Approved 140W Power Bank"**, you probably expected to recharge your dead laptop nearly from 0% to 100%.

Instead, your power bank hits **0%** while your MacBook battery gauge sits at **71%**—or worse, the power bank's screen flashes an over-temperature warning halfway through the transfer and drops output to zero while its plastic shell radiates heat inside your carry-on bag.

Why does a "99.5Wh" battery never deliver 99.5Wh to your laptop, and why do some $160 packs choke when you actually pull their advertised 140 watts? We hooked today's most popular airline-legal laptop power banks up to our programmable constant-power DC load and thermal logger to separate real engineering from spec-sheet fiction.

## Trap #1: The "27,650mAh" Voltage Trick (Why mAh Is Meaningless for USB-C PD)

Smartphone and laptop buyers have been conditioned for fifteen years to compare batteries by **milliamp-hours (`mAh`)**. Because a MacBook Pro 16-inch battery is around `8,693mAh` (at its nominal `11.45V` pack architecture), a traveler sees **"27,650mAh"** on a power bank box and assumes it holds more than three full MacBook charges.

It holds less than one. Here is the physics that brands bury in fine print:

- **Power banks quote `mAh` at the single-cell voltage (`3.6V` or `3.7V`)**, even when the six internal cylindrical cells are wired in a `6S` (`21.6V`) series string.
- $$\text{99.54 Wh} = \frac{27,650\text{ mAh} \times 3.6\text{ V}}{1000}$$
- When your MacBook Pro negotiates a **20V** (`100W`) or **28V** (`140W PD 3.1 EPR`) charging contract, the current delivered at 28V is equivalent to just **`3,555 mAh` at 28V** before conversion losses.

Never compare USB-C power banks by `mAh`. Always flip the pack over, read the **Watt-hour (`Wh`)** stamp required by FAA/TSA aviation rules (capped at `100Wh` for carry-on bags without airline approval), and multiply that number by **`0.78`**.

## Trap #2: Where the Missing 21 to 35 Watt-Hours Actually Go

In our constant-power `100W (20V/5A)` discharge bench, even the highest-scoring pack in our lab—the **Anker Prime 27,650mAh (`99.54Wh` rated)**—delivered **78.4Wh** at the USB-C receptacle (`78.8%` end-to-end efficiency). Where did the other `21.1Wh` disappear?

1. **Internal Cell Impedance (`I²R` Heating)**: Pulling 100W to 140W out of six 21700 cells requires **6A to 8.5A of cell current**. As the cells discharge from `4.2V` down toward `3.1V`, internal resistance converts roughly **7% to 9%** of stored chemical energy directly into cell heat.
2. **Bidirectional Buck-Boost Converter Losses**: Stepping a sagging 6-series battery pack (`18.6V` near empty) up to a rock-steady **28.0V at 5.0A** (`140W`) forces the synchronous MOSFET H-bridge and alloy power inductor to work at peak switching stress, burning another **10% to 14%** as board heat.
3. **Your Laptop's Internal PMIC Loss**: Once that `78.4Wh` enters your MacBook's USB-C or MagSafe 3 port, the laptop's own internal charging regulator loses another ~6% charging its internal pouch cells—yielding roughly a **68% to 72% real battery refill** on a 16-inch MacBook Pro, or a **100% full charge plus 15% to spare** on a 14-inch MacBook Pro (`72.4Wh` battery).

## Trap #3: The 140W Thermal Cutoff Cliff on Clear Plastic "Cyber" Packs

Over the past year, transparent polycarbonate "cyberpunk" power banks exposing painted yellow circuitry have flooded social feeds at **$159 to $199**.

Clear polycarbonate has terrible thermal conductivity (`~0.19 W/m·K`), and to keep the PCB visible through the window, designers cannot press a full-length aluminum heatsink plate across the top of the buck-boost MOSFETs and inductor.

When we ran a continuous **140W (28V/5A)** discharge test on a popular clear-shell 93.4Wh pack:

- At **Minute 14 (52% battery remaining)**, our thermocouple inside the clear housing hit **88.6°C** on the main boost inductor.
- The Battery Management System (`BMS`) immediately tripped over-temperature protection, dropping the USB-C port from `140W` to `0W` for 40 seconds before re-negotiating a throttled `45W` profile.
- By contrast, the **Ugreen Nexode 25,000mAh (`66.8°C`)** and **Anker Prime 27,650mAh (`69.4°C`)** use thick internal aluminum heat-spreader cages and high-discharge rate 21700 cells, sustaining full high-wattage output virtually all the way to empty.

## The Buying Rules for Flight-Safe Laptop Power in 2026

- **Use the `Wh × 0.72` Rule to size your pack**: A `99.5Wh` airline-max power bank puts about **71Wh of net charge** into your device's battery. That equals **0.72× charges** for a 16-inch MacBook Pro (`99.6Wh`), **1.0× full charge** for a 14-inch MacBook Pro (`72.4Wh`), **1.9× charges** for an 11-inch/13-inch iPad Pro (`36.6Wh`), or **4.2× charges** for an iPhone 16 Pro.
