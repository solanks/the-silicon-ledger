**Every iPad Pro commercial shows a creative professional effortlessly replacing their laptop with a razor-thin tablet snapped onto a floating keyboard. What the commercial leaves out is the checkout receipt—and what happens when you put both machines on a digital postal scale.**

Start with a 13-inch iPad Pro at **\$1,299**. Add the mandatory aluminum Magic Keyboard (**\$349**) and the Apple Pencil Pro (**\$129**), and your "ultralight tablet setup" just hit **\$1,777 before tax**—more than a 14-inch MacBook Pro.

Even more absurdly, when we placed the 13-inch iPad Pro + Magic Keyboard combo on our lab scale, it weighed **2.82 pounds (1,280 grams)**—making it **heavier** than a 13-inch MacBook Air (2.7 pounds) while offering half the battery life under keyboard-and-trackpad productivity loads.

## Why Apple Refuses to Put macOS on the iPad Pro

Hardware is no longer the limitation. The silicon inside an iPad Pro is identical in architecture to the chip powering a MacBook Air and Mac mini—meaning it could boot full macOS Sequoia in eight seconds if Apple's executives allowed it.

Why won't they? Because if a single \$1,299 device could serve as a touch tablet on the couch and boot into full macOS when docked to a keyboard, millions of customers would stop buying **both** an iPad and a MacBook.

Until iPadOS allows true background daemon processes, unrestricted terminal/compiler access, and a desktop-grade Finder file system, buying an iPad Pro to do laptop work is paying a \$500 premium to work with one hand tied behind your back.

## How to Buy an iPad Without Getting Fleeced

If you want the Tandem OLED screen for movies, illustration, and travel reading, buy the **11-inch iPad Pro on Amazon sale (\$899)** and pair it with a \$35 magnetic folio cover—never the \$349 Magic Keyboard. If you actually need to type for four hours a day, buy a **MacBook Air** and save yourself \$750.
